Phase6D weekly evidence trends (#29): Settings → Weekly trends compares two explicitly dated weeks (Monday–Sunday in the snapshot timezone, by source `generatedAt`; import time shown separately) through a read-only, bounded `GET /api/trends`. It shows observed required-work counts by status and per-source health only. Weeks without observations say "No observations", never 0. Records from failed/unavailable sources, archived records and duplicate imports are not counted as fresh. Any non-ok or unreported source, different source sets in the two latest observations, retained evidence the bounded read did not return, a week reaching the 30-day retention horizon, or a truncated read makes the pair "Not comparable", with no change shown. Weeks whose evidence was not read say "No observations read", not "No observations". "No observations" only means none was found within the bounded reads (500 rows, the 1,000 most recent imports and 30-day retention); it is missing data, not proof that nothing was collected. Snapshot pruning and the schema are unchanged. No effort, completion times or work sessions are inferred. See the [trends contract](data-contract.md#weekly-evidence-trends-phase-6d-29).

Approved Google Calendar bridge (DW-GOOGLE-01, task 3): Q versions bind provider, account, calendar ID, every event field and source task refs. Google actions are one-time personal creates on an owner calendar, with no attendees, invitations or meetings. `scripts/google-calendar.cjs` (local, no credentials or network) targets the connected create tool's declared arguments (no invitation-update setting, client event ID or extended properties, so duplicate protection is the description marker, the serial pending receipt and reconciliation), verifies the human's message is exactly "Approve Q-N" and every required argument is declared, checks a per-calendar live re-read at most 15 minutes old for conflicts, duplicates, unreadable times and coverage, records pending before the single write, compares the connector's raw read-back field by field against the queried calendar (executed or unverified) and reconciles unknown outcomes without retrying: every marked event a claim sees (any outcome, any calendar, also in an incomplete read or with unreadable times; identity is calendar and event ID) stays on its receipt, and two or more distinct ones, seen by one search or across several, hold the action until a complete reconcile finds exactly one while covering the recorded windows, calendars and matches (at least 24 hours either side); with one seen, only that event is read back. Anything seen that could not be recorded, an unreadable scope, or an outcome recorded before every outcome kept its marked events makes the range unknown: it never clears automatically and needs the human, with no command to record that yet. not_created needs a claim that saw no marked event and complete reads 24 hours either side of the action, and claims absence only there, and a not-created action needs a newly approved version; every version it was not created under stays refused for new pending claims through later attempts, restore and import, while a claim already started still records its outcome (#46). The tool is the documented receipt path: legacy receipt commands refuse Google receipts, and snapshot imports are rejected when they carry a Google-shaped receipt the local journal lacks or, while either coverage is Google, any new receipt not belonging to an Outlook proposal already stored locally; local receipts always win. These are consistency guards, not authentication; local code, database access or a placed backup file can still write receipts. Supervising launcher (DW-1006): `scripts/launcher.cjs` is portable, dependency-free tooling that starts the app for one pinned checkout ([guide](launcher.md)). Ownership is an atomic lock plus a challenge-response proof over the owner's local channel; a PID, an HTTP 200 or a banner is never identity. Simultaneous starts give exactly one owner, and its app process reserves the port before the Store opens, so refused or losing launchers never touch data. Foreign listeners, unverifiable or possibly live holders and identity mismatches (revision, paths, port, config, manifest, launcher) fail closed with fixed exit codes and nothing is terminated; a lock is recovered only when its owner channel and its recorded child are provably gone (a child keeps its beacon until its process has exited, also while still closing). Diagnostics fall back from the primary log through fallback logs and a per-user temp file to stderr, and the fatal handler cannot throw. Failed app processes (crash, startup failure or timeout, missed heartbeat) are restarted within a bounded budget with retained history; any config, revision, checkout or port change stops recovery instead. `scripts/serve.cjs` now builds the app through `src/app-server.cjs` with unchanged behavior; for a revision without that file the launcher falls back to `attach(createServer())`, which matches only revisions whose `serve.cjs` assembles the app that way (other older revisions are unsupported). Evidence is synthetic subprocess tests on Windows and in the Linux CI job. The stable runtime still uses its private legacy launcher; adoption needs separate approval, and real sign-in, sign-out, reboot, scheduler restart-on-failure and recovery of the real service are not tested.
